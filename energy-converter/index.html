Exact conversion factors

These are the definitions the calculator uses. Nothing here is rounded for convenience — where a value is exact by definition, it says so.

  • 1 kcal (food Calorie) = 4184 J = 4.184 kJ
  • 1 kWh = 3 600 000 J = 3.6 MJ = 860.420650 kcal
  • 1 Wh = 3600 J · 1 BTU = 1055.05585262 J
  • 1 ft·lbf = 1.35581794833 J · 1 eV = 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

How this converter works

The food calorie is the classic source of confusion. What a label calls a Calorie is a kilocalorie: 4184 J. The small calorie used in chemistry is a thousand times smaller. Both rows are here, labelled plainly, so nothing is guessed.

On the electricity side, one kilowatt-hour is 3.6 MJ. That makes bill arithmetic easy: a 2 kW heater running three hours uses 6 kWh, or 21.6 MJ, and at typical tariffs you can price it straight from the kWh row.

Notes and abbreviations

  • 1 kcal = 4184 J — the "Calorie" printed on food packaging
  • 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ = 860.42 kcal
  • 1 BTU = 1055.06 J — air conditioning and boiler ratings
  • eV — electronvolt, the unit of choice in particle and semiconductor physics

Questions

How many joules is one food calorie?

One food Calorie is one kilocalorie, or 4184 J. A 500 kcal meal is 2.092 MJ.

How much energy is a kWh?

3 600 000 J. Enough to run a 100 W bulb for ten hours.

Can I convert BTU to kW for an air conditioner?

BTU ratings on air conditioners are per hour, so use the power converter: 12 000 BTU/h is 3.517 kW.
