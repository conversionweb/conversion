How this converter works

The conversions people reach for most: 100 km/h is 62.14 mph, 60 mph is 96.56 km/h, and 1 m/s is 3.6 km/h exactly. The last one is worth memorising, since it turns most physics answers into everyday numbers in one step.

Knots come from sea navigation — one nautical mile per hour, or 1.852 km/h — and are still standard in aviation and marine forecasts. Mach is included as a reference at 340.29 m/s, the speed of sound in dry air at 15 °C at sea level; it drops with temperature, so treat it as an approximation rather than a fixed conversion.

Notes and abbreviations

  • kn — knot, one nautical mile per hour, 1.852 km/h
  • 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h exactly; 1 mph = 1.609344 km/h exactly
  • Mach 1 varies with air temperature — the value here is the sea-level standard

Questions

What is 100 km/h in mph?

62.1371 mph. Going the other way, 70 mph is 112.654 km/h.

How do I convert m/s to km/h in my head?

Multiply by 3.6. A 10 m/s wind is a 36 km/h wind.

Why is Mach not a fixed speed?

It is a ratio to the local speed of sound, which falls as air gets colder. At cruising altitude Mach 1 is roughly 295 m/s, not 340.
