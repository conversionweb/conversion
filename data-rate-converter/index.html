Data transfer rate converter — Mbit/s, MB/s and GB/h

Internet plans are sold in megabits, download windows count in megabytes, and the gap between them is a factor of eight. This page shows both at once, plus how much a link moves per hour.

How to use it

  1. 1

    Type into any row — say Mbit/s or MB/s. The results appear immediately, no button needed.

  2. 2

    Need a combined figure? Fill in two rows, for example Mbit/s and Gbit/s, and they are added together before converting.

  3. 3

    Set how many digits you want (4–12) and click a result to copy the plain number.

Exact conversion factors

These are the definitions the calculator uses. Nothing here is rounded for convenience — where a value is exact by definition, it says so.

  • 1 byte = 8 bits, so 1 MB/s = 8 Mbit/s
  • 100 Mbit/s = 12.5 MB/s = 45 GB per hour
  • 1 Gbit/s = 125 MB/s · 1 Gbit/s = 450 GB per hour
  • 1 MiB/s = 8.388608 Mbit/s — binary megabyte, 4.86 percent larger
  • A 4 GB file over 100 Mbit/s takes 320 s at full speed
FormulaMB/s = Mbit/s ÷ 8 · Mbit/s = MB/s × 8 · GB/h = GB/s × 3600 · MiB/s = MB/s × 0.95367432

How this converter works

Providers advertise in bits because the number looks eight times bigger. A "100 megabit" line delivers 12.5 MB/s at best, and real transfers land lower once protocol overhead, Wi-Fi and the far end are taken into account — treat these results as the ceiling, not the promise.

The GB per hour row answers the planning question directly: how much can this link actually shift overnight? A 50 Mbit/s upload moves 22.5 GB in an hour, so a 200 GB backup needs about nine hours of clean uptime.

Notes and abbreviations

  • Lowercase b means bit, uppercase B means byte — 8 bits
  • Mbps and Mbit/s are the same thing; MBps and MB/s are eight times faster
  • MiB/s uses the binary megabyte of 1 048 576 bytes

Questions

How fast is 100 Mbps in MB/s?

12.5 MB/s. Divide any bit rate by 8 to get the byte rate.

How long will a 25 GB download take on 200 Mbit/s?

200 Mbit/s is 25 MB/s, so 1000 seconds — about 17 minutes at full speed.

Why is my download slower than the advertised speed?

Protocol overhead, Wi-Fi losses and the server at the other end all cut into it. The figures here are the theoretical maximum.
