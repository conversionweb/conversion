Exact conversion factors

These are the definitions the calculator uses. Nothing here is rounded for convenience — where a value is exact by definition, it says so.

  • 1 bar = 100 000 Pa = 100 kPa = 14.5037738 psi
  • 1 atm = 101 325 Pa exactly = 1.01325 bar = 14.6959488 psi = 760 Torr (759.99989 mmHg)
  • 1 psi = 6894.757293168 Pa = 0.0689475729 bar
  • 1 mmHg = 133.322387415 Pa · 1 inHg = 3386.388640341 Pa
  • 1 hPa = 1 mbar — identical sizes

How this converter works

Two numbers cover most everyday work: 1 bar is 14.5 psi, and standard atmospheric pressure is 1013.25 mbar or 14.7 psi. A car tyre at 2.2 bar is sitting at 31.9 psi, which is why both scales appear on garage gauges.

Everything on the page is gauge-independent — it converts the number you type, not the reference it was measured against. If your source reads in absolute pressure (bara, psia) and your target expects gauge (barg, psig), subtract one atmosphere before or after converting; the converter cannot know which you meant.

Notes and abbreviations

  • 1 bar = 100 kPa = 14.504 psi
  • 1 atm = 101.325 kPa = 760 mmHg = 14.696 psi
  • mbar and hPa are the same size — weather maps use them interchangeably

Questions

What tyre pressure is 32 psi in bar?

2.2063 bar. Going back, 2.5 bar is 36.2594 psi.

Are hPa and mbar the same?

Yes, exactly. A reading of 1013 hPa and 1013 mbar describe identical pressure.

Does this handle absolute versus gauge pressure?

It converts magnitudes only. For gauge to absolute, add 1.01325 bar yourself.
