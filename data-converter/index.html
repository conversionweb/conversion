These are the definitions the calculator uses. Nothing here is rounded for convenience — where a value is exact by definition, it says so.

How this converter works

This is the page that explains the missing space on your hard drive. Manufacturers sell a terabyte as 1 000 000 000 000 bytes, while Windows reports capacity in tebibytes of 1024⁴ bytes and calls them TB. The same drive is 1 TB and 0.909 TiB — nothing has vanished, the labels just disagree.

Bits are here too, one eighth of a byte, which matters whenever network speed meets file size. A 100 Mbit/s connection moves 12.5 MB per second at best, so a 4 GB download needs at least five and a half minutes even on a perfect line.

Notes and abbreviations

  • Decimal: kB = 1000 B, MB = 1000 kB, GB = 1000 MB
  • Binary: KiB = 1024 B, MiB = 1024 KiB, GiB = 1024 MiB
  • 1 byte = 8 bits — network speeds are in bits, file sizes in bytes

Questions

Why does my 1 TB drive show 931 GB?

The drive holds 10¹² bytes. The operating system divides by 1024 three times and prints 931 — those are really gibibytes.

How long does a 4 GB file take on 100 Mbit/s?

4 GB is 32 000 megabits, so 320 seconds at full speed — five minutes twenty.

Is MB the same as MiB?

No. 1 MiB is 1 048 576 bytes, 4.86 percent more than 1 MB.
